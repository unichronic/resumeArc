# Startup Outreach Tiers

Based on: [startup_research.md](/home/unichronic/intern/startup_research.md)

Research date used: 2026-04-25

## How I split these

This is an effort-allocation decision, not a prestige ranking.

1. `Research + build a product proof of work + cold mail`
Use this when the company is young enough to value initiative, technical enough to appreciate a concrete artifact, and has strong public signals of momentum, urgency, or founder-led hiring.

2. `Tailor resume + cold mail`
Use this when the company looks real and worth targeting, but the expected return on a custom proof of work is lower because the company is more structured, more domain-heavy, more regulated, more hardware-heavy, or already hiring through normal channels.

3. `Cold mail only / shoot your shot`
Use this when hiring signals are weak, founder accessibility is unclear, the company looks too early or too unclear for heavy prep, or the likely ROI of deep customization is low relative to your time.

Extra filter applied:
- If a company has existed for a while and the team is still very small, I treat that as a negative hiring-intent signal by default.
- I only override that if there is strong counter-evidence such as explicit current openings, fresh funding, strong traction, or visible founder-led recruiting.
- If a company is not older than 2 years, is still small, and raised within roughly the last 3 months, I treat that as a positive hiring-intent signal because the small size is still normal for that stage.

## 1. Research + Build a Proof of Work + Cold Mail

- Monk: Fresh Series A, real customer outcomes, small team, and workflow/product surface is concrete enough to demo collections automation ideas.
- Blackstar: Tiny team, very early, multiple open roles, and founder-led hiring; a sharp systems/product concept note or prototype could stand out more than a standard application.
- Solidroad: Small, high-growth, clear customer support AI problem, and strong traction make a domain-specific QA/agent demo worth the effort.
- OpenRouter: Product is API-native, highly technical, and founder/startup friendly; a concrete build on top of their stack is exactly the kind of signal they can evaluate quickly.
- Dex: Extremely small team and founding-style roles; a product/ops agent prototype is a stronger signal than a resume alone.
- Pulse: Still small and recent enough that a workflow/eval-heavy demo can beat a normal application if it is tightly scoped to document AI.
- SubImage: Early open-core/open-source style company; contributions or a security/cartography-adjacent proof of work fit the company shape.
- Browser Use: Open-source traction is massive and founder-led hiring is visible; code, integrations, or agent demos are likely higher ROI than pure cold outreach.
- Knowlify: Very early, technical, and product-led; a doc-to-video workflow demo or API integration is the strongest possible outreach asset.
- SigNoz: Open-source/devtool company where shipping something on top of the stack or contributing is materially stronger than a generic resume.
- CodeRabbit: Startup-style hiring is active, the team is still small enough to care about initiative, and an AI code-review workflow demo maps directly to the product.

## 2. Tailor Resume + Cold Mail

- Blossom Health: Real traction and hiring, but healthcare compliance and operations make a lightweight proof of work less reliable than a tightly tailored application.
- Bloom (now Plura): Founded in 2025 and publicly hiring, so this is too real for a throwaway mail, but the niche consumer/community angle still does not justify proof-of-work-first.
- Mintlify: Excellent company, but at 55 people with strong public traction and a more structured hiring funnel, a sharply tailored application is safer than speculative build-first outreach.
- Pogo: Good traction and consumer scale, but it has been around since 2020 and is no longer small enough to justify a speculative proof of work over a focused application.
- Round Treasury: Good target, but treasury/fintech trust makes domain fit and thoughtful positioning more reliable than a speculative product build for most candidates.
- Avec: Young and recently funded, but this is still consumer/product-led enough that a tight narrative and tailored resume are safer than overinvesting in a demo.
- Blip AI: Small AI dictation product with founder visibility, but the hiring signal is thin and the stack appears cloud/API-based rather than on-device. Tailor around desktop voice UX, speech-to-text latency, model-provider costs, privacy claims, and offline/on-device fallback instead of building a large proof of work.
- Auctor: Real hiring and a clear workflow product, but at 25 people with enterprise buyers it now reads better as a tailored-application target than a proof-of-work-first one.
- SuperKalam: Worth targeting, especially if relevant to edtech/India; tailoring to product, growth, or student-engagement work is likely enough.
- Swif.ai: Legit company with revenue and YC backing, but device/security workflows are specialized enough that resume tailoring is safer than speculative build work.
- SID: The product is technical, but founded in 2022 and still only 2-10 people means the safer read is selective hiring rather than broad hiring intent.
- Nester: Founded in 2025 and marked publicly hiring, so it should not sit in the lowest bucket, but the consumer-appliance angle still makes resume tailoring a better use of time than building a proof of work.
- NeoDocs: Strong commercial traction and public hiring, but healthtech context makes domain fit and resume positioning more important than a generic prototype.
- Novaflow: Very interesting, but bioinformatics is specialized enough that proof-of-work only pays off if you already have domain credibility; for most candidates, tailoring is safer.
- TsecondAI: Deeptech/hardware/defense is usually evaluation-heavy and domain-constrained; tailor hard, then cold mail.
- Digital Paani: Strong customer logos and recent funding, but infra/industrial deployment work is domain-specific and less conducive to quick proof-of-work wins.
- Lawyered: Real company and current scaling signals, but legal-tech has trust/process constraints that make resume positioning more efficient.
- Spill Games: Positive unit economics and fresh seed make it worth targeting, but game studios often evaluate by prior fit and shipped work rather than a bespoke cold-mail artifact.
- CureMeAbroad: Founded in 2025, funded in the last few days of the research window, and marked publicly hiring; that is enough to justify a tailored application, but not enough to justify proof-of-work-first.
- GobbleCube: Strong momentum and active hiring make it worth targeting, but at 130+ people it is already structured enough that a tailored application is higher ROI than speculative build work.
- Atlas: Fresh seed and founded in 2025 are positive signals, but there is still no clear public hiring evidence, so this belongs in the middle rather than the highest-effort bucket.
- Work On Grid: Founded in 2017 and still relatively small, so I discount broad hiring intent, but the fresh round and active careers page keep it worth a tailored approach.
- Deccan AI: Strong company, but 101-250 employees and an eight-figure revenue run rate point to a more structured hiring process where tailored positioning is more defensible than proof-of-work-first.
- CreativeMode: Small and real, but this is better treated like a portfolio/fit-driven gaming-tools target than a generic proof-of-work-first outreach case.
- CAST AI: Strong company with current hiring and recent capital, but at 101-250 people in enterprise infra it should be approached like a growth-stage company, not a tiny founder-led team.
- ClickHouse: Excellent company, but now too structured and too large for speculative proof-of-work to clearly beat a strong targeted application.
- Chainguard: Strong security/open-source company with active hiring, but company maturity and security-domain hiring norms make resume tailoring the safer high-ROI move.
- CockroachDB: Worth targeting if your systems/database fit is strong, but the company is mature enough that focused positioning should beat custom cold-build effort.

- TiDB: Strong infra/database target if relevant to your background, but this should be pursued through tailored positioning rather than custom speculative demos.
- YugabyteDB: Similar to TiDB and CockroachDB; good target for distributed systems fit, but not a proof-of-work-first outreach case.
- NeoSOFT Cloud: Real hiring signal exists, but it is services-led enough that tailored resume positioning should dominate.
- TestMu AI: Active hiring and strong product traction, but at 201-500 employees it belongs in the middle bucket rather than the founder-led one.
- OceanBase: Real database/infra company with active hiring, but founder-led cold-outreach leverage appears limited, so tailor rather than build first.
- Apica: Observability company with active hiring; viable target, but scale and product maturity push it into tailored-application territory.
- Azul: Established JVM company with active hiring; worth tailoring for if relevant, but not worth custom speculative build effort first.

## 3. Cold Mail Only / Shoot Your Shot

- Scalify: Very small and likely bootstrapped, but hiring signal is weak; reach out directly without spending much build time first.
- MinitPe: Very early and small, with limited public structure; a concise cold mail is enough.
- Umero: Founder visibility is weak and the company is still early-access; not worth deep prep until engagement signal improves.
- Solution Crow: Services business with no clear hiring funnel; low upside for heavy customization.
- SatLeo Labs: Technically ambitious, but public hiring signal is weak and the domain is specialized enough that speculative work has low expected ROI.
- Lawyered if hiring evidence cools off: founded in 2018 and still not clearly scaled, so this should be watched as a possible downgrade if the active-scaling signal weakens.
- Revvity: Not a startup, hiring is corporate, and cold outreach should be low-effort unless you are targeting a very specific team.
- Rackspace Tech: Similar logic to Revvity; large-company funnel dynamics reduce the payoff from custom build work.
- Concerto by Trianz: Enterprise services/platform business with 1,000-2,000 employees; normal hiring likely goes through formal channels, so custom prep has weak marginal payoff.
- Redis: Excellent company, but already mature enough that generic cold outreach has low odds unless you have a very specific referral or OSS contribution trail.
- ManageEngine: Large, profitable, structured Zoho division; treat this like formal hiring, not startup-style campaign work.
- Xurrent: Older ITSM company with limited founder-outreach leverage; a short direct note is fine, but deep prep is hard to justify.
- Zarthi: Services/procurement business with sparse hiring metadata; reach out only if a clear role appears.
- AppSquadz: Services company with 500+ experts; low leverage for heavy startup-style proof-of-work outreach.
- IBM Cloudability: This is a product line inside IBM/Apptio, so startup-style cold outreach is low-yield.
- StackGen: Interesting infra startup, but the current table is still too sparse on hiring and team shape to justify more than a lightweight shot right now.
- CloudThat Technologies Pvt. Ltd.: Training/consulting business with formal hiring and partner-led credibility; low payoff for bespoke outreach.
- Cloud.in: Metadata is too sparse to justify real prep.
- i2k2 Networks Pvt. Limited: Managed services company with formal careers flow; treat as low-effort outreach only.
- Miniorange: Real company, but now too established and structured for deep speculative outreach to pay off well.
- Shellkode: Services-led firm with sparse hiring metadata; reach out only if you see a direct need or role.
- Zeliot: Public metadata is too sparse in the current table to justify more than a lightweight shot.
- Trainocate: Training provider, not a startup-style target for product proof-of-work.

## Borderline Cases

- Solution Crow: Founded in 2020, still very small, no major funding signal, and no public hiring found. That looks more like a stable small services business than a company actively trying to scale headcount, so it stays in `cold mail only`.
- Atlas: The age-and-funding signal is positive, but the missing public hiring signal keeps it out of the top-effort bucket.
- GobbleCube: Strong enough to target seriously, but too structured now to justify proof-of-work-first.
- Deccan AI: Strong hiring case, but better approached like a scaled growth-stage company than a tiny founder-led team.
- CodeRabbit: One of the few new additions that still cleanly fits the top bucket because the team is small, the hiring style is startup-like, and the product is directly demo-able.
- Dragonfly: I now treat this as middle-bucket by default; only push it to top-bucket if you can send a genuinely strong infra/database artifact.
- Mintlify: Strong target, but no longer a default Tier 1 because company maturity and hiring structure reduce the marginal payoff of speculative build-first outreach.
- Novaflow: Move back to Tier 1 only if you already have bioinformatics or computational biology evidence.
- CreativeMode: Move back to Tier 1 only if you can send a genuinely impressive mod/tooling portfolio artifact.
